Helps with legal work across contracts and other legal documents. Draft new documents, understand and review content, make precise edits, and surface key information and obligations.

Responses are generated by AI and may contain mistakes. Important note: Microsoft 365 Copilot does not provide legal advice or replace qualified legal judgment. Over-reliance on unverified outputs may result in legal, commercial, or compliance risk. You are solely responsible for verifying any output before acting on it.

> **Cowork plugin.** This is a Microsoft 365 Copilot **Cowork** app package (a `.zip` bundling the skills and connectors below). It installs on Cowork only.

## Skills in this plugin

- **analyze-related-contracts** — Analyzes how a set of contracts relate and produces a Related Contracts Report: which document amends, restates, replaces or terminates which, the effective sequence, and the currently operative version of each key term with its governing clause. Read-only on sources. Use when the user asks "how do these contracts relate", "which amendment is currently operative", "build a related contracts report", "map this contract family", "which version of this term applies now", or supplies amendments, restatements, SOWs, variations or novations and asks what is still in force. Do NOT use for one agreement family answered inline in chat — use legal-reasoning instead; for the same fields compared across documents use compare-key-terms, and for duties and dates use extract-obligations-and-deadlines.
- **build-case-chronology** — Builds a dated, source-cited chronology of factual and procedural events from supplied case papers, with date flags, evidence pinpoints and mandatory Assumptions & Discrepancies and Coverage sections. Read-only on sources; it never assesses merits, liability or limitation. Use when the user asks to "build a case chronology", "create a litigation timeline", "prepare a chronology for this dispute", "list the procedural deadlines in this matter", or "work out who did what and when across these papers". Do NOT use for contractual obligations and deadlines taken from an agreement — use extract-obligations-and-deadlines instead; for matter activity drawn from email, Teams and calendar use summarize-legal-matters, and for a reasoned view on a legal question use legal-reasoning.
- **check-cross-references** — Checks every internal cross-reference in a supplied legal document against an index of its actual clause, schedule, annex and exhibit numbering, and reports each outdated, missing, mislabelled, ambiguous or unverifiable reference with a quoted citation plus suggested wording for the mechanical fixes. Use when the user asks to "check the cross-references in this agreement", "are the clause references in this contract still correct?", "find broken or outdated internal references in this document", or "which references can't be verified without the other agreements?". Do NOT use for undefined, duplicated or inconsistently used defined terms — use check-defined-terms instead; for blanks and open tracked changes before signing use check-execution-readiness, and for what a referenced clause means use legal-reasoning.
- **check-defined-terms** — Audits the defined term machinery of a supplied legal document — terms used but not defined, defined but not used, defined twice, used inconsistently, or resting on an undefined term — reporting each defect with a quoted citation to its clause, then suggested wording the user applies. Read-only. Use when the user asks to "check the defined terms in this NDA", "any duplicate definitions?", "is Confidential Information used consistently?", "find any terms used but not defined in this agreement", or "is anything defined but never used?". Do NOT use for broken clause, schedule or annex references — use check-cross-references instead; for blanks and open tracked changes before signing use check-execution-readiness, and for what a clause means use legal-reasoning.
- **check-execution-readiness** — Read-only check of whether a legal document is complete enough to sign: party names and roles, execution and effective dates, signature blocks, and unresolved drafting such as blanks, placeholders, TBD markers and open tracked changes. Gives one position plus a Finding / Location / What is needed table, and never proposes wording. Use when the user asks "is this ready to sign", "run an execution readiness check", "check the signature blocks", "are there any blanks left in this agreement", or "can we execute this today". Do NOT use for a defined term or cross-reference audit — use check-defined-terms or check-cross-references instead; to explain what open tracked changes say use summarize-redlines, and for the position across an amended contract family use analyze-related-contracts.
- **compare-across-jurisdictions** — Produces a cited side-by-side comparison of one legal question across two or more named jurisdictions, using a framework frozen before research, one evidence status per cell (such as Stated or Not located) and one divergence label per row. Neutral comparative research for counsel review — never legal advice, a forum recommendation or an outcome prediction. Use when the user asks to "compare paid leave rules across these states", "how is this point litigated in France versus England", "what does each country say about X", "prepare a per-jurisdiction position table", or "where do these jurisdictions diverge". Do NOT use to test a document against legal or regulatory standards — use identify-regulatory-gaps instead; to compare contract wording use compare-key-terms, and a single-jurisdiction answer is legal-reasoning.
- **compare-key-terms** — Extracts one consistent schema of legal and commercial fields across several documents and delivers a source-linked comparison in chat, a Word document, or an Excel grid, every value carrying its exact quote and clause locator. Use when the user asks for "a key terms spreadsheet for these contracts", "a contract abstract of this portfolio", "compare these agreements side by side", "pull the same fields out of each of these NDAs", "build me a review grid from these documents", or "review these contracts against my column list". Do NOT use for who-must-do-what-by-when duty and date tracking — use extract-obligations-and-deadlines instead; for a document against legislation or policy use identify-regulatory-gaps.
- **create-playbook** — Derives a contract review playbook from a set of precedent agreements — signed deals or drafts carrying your side's negotiation markup — recurring positions, accepted alternatives and counsel decisions — and delivers it as a four-column Word table. This is review guidance synthesised from many precedents, never a new agreement drafted from one. Use when the user asks to "build a playbook from these contracts", "derive our standard positions from precedent", "create a negotiation guide from these signed agreements", "what positions do our approved MSAs support", or "turn these precedents into review guidance". Do NOT use to review or redline one contract against a playbook — use review-contract instead; for field-by-field extraction across documents use compare-key-terms.
- **draft-new-contract** — Turns ONE precedent agreement into a new working draft as an editable Word document (.docx), replacing every party-specific and transaction-specific detail with a visible placeholder — then, optionally and often later, fills those placeholders with values grounded in the user's own instructions, email and files. Use when the user asks to "draft an NDA based on this one", "use this MSA as a precedent for the Acme deal", "turn this into a new agreement for the new supplier", "templatise this agreement", or "fill in the blanks on that draft". Do NOT use to derive review guidance or standard positions from many approved precedents — use create-playbook instead; for questions about what a document says use legal-reasoning.
- **draft-legal-memo** — Drafts a source-grounded legal memorandum, in chat or as a Word document, answering one defined legal question from supplied matter documents for a named represented party and audience. Every statement is anchored to a clause, section or cited authority, and the memo is always marked a draft for counsel review. Use when the user says "draft a legal memo", "write a memorandum of advice", "give me a written legal analysis of this contract", "prepare a memo on whether we can terminate", or "advise me in writing on this dispute". Do NOT use for drafting or redlining clause wording (use edit-contract), for producing a new agreement from a precedent (use draft-new-contract), or for a short reasoned answer with no memo deliverable (use legal-reasoning).
- **draft-minutes-and-resolutions** — Turns a board, committee, trustee or governance meeting transcript into formal draft minutes and written resolutions in the body's house format, with every outcome classified and anchored to a speaker and timestamp. Use when the user asks to "draft minutes from this transcript", "write up the board meeting", "turn this recording into minutes", "prepare the committee resolutions", or "produce minutes in our house format". Do NOT use for matter activity gathered from mail, Teams and calendar — use summarize-legal-matters instead; for dated events reconstructed from case papers use build-case-chronology; for a governance question answered for a lawyer use legal-reasoning.
- **explain-legal-concepts** — Explains what a contract or legal concept actually says in everyday language for a NON-LEGAL reader, keeping every number, deadline, condition, owner and clause reference intact, and delivers it in chat or as an email or Teams draft. Use when the user asks to "explain this contract in plain English", "translate this agreement for the deal team", "summarise this NDA for the business", "what do we have to do under this deal?", or "draft an email explaining these terms to sales". Do NOT use for a reasoned legal answer written for a lawyer — use legal-reasoning instead, and route here only when the audience is non-legal; for a same-fields comparison grid use compare-key-terms; for a document tested against legislation or policy use identify-regulatory-gaps.
- **extract-obligations-and-deadlines** — Extracts obligations, rights, deadlines, triggers and stated consequences from supplied agreements into a source-linked tracker, one row per distinct action, delivered in chat, Excel or Word. Use when the user asks to "pull the obligations and key dates out of these agreements", "build an obligations tracker from this MSA", "list who must do what, by when and on what trigger", "make a deadline register from these contracts", or "consolidate these agreements into one tracker before renewal". Do NOT use for extracting the same comparison fields across a portfolio — use compare-key-terms instead; for a dated narrative of what happened in a dispute use build-case-chronology.
- **identify-regulatory-gaps** — Identifies gaps against a standard: builds a requirement inventory from retrieved legislation, regulator rules, standards or internal policy text, traces every requirement into every supplied document, and reports each pair in one state with citations on both sides. Use when the user asks to "check this against the new regulations", "run a gap analysis against the rulebook", "does this policy meet the guidance?", "which live deals does this rule change affect?", "check these contracts against our internal policy", or "which of our agreements cite the old article?". Do NOT use for the same fields across contracts — use compare-key-terms instead; for explaining a regulation with no document to test use Cowork's built-in web research.
- **improve-legal-prompt** — Turns a rough request or an existing draft into one ready-to-use Cowork prompt, preserving the user's original legal objective while making document scope, party perspective, constraints, missing information handling and expected output explicit. Returns the prompt plus two or three improvement notes, and never runs it. Use when the user asks to "optimize my prompt", "rewrite my prompt for reviewing this agreement", "improve this prompt", "how should I ask Cowork to revise the termination clause", "clarify this instruction before I run it", or "why isn't my prompt working". Do NOT use for the legal work itself — "rewrite this clause" or "make this paragraph clearer" is edit-contract, a reasoned answer on a legal question is legal-reasoning, and reviewing an agreement is review-contract.
- **edit-contract** — Makes precise, minimal changes to an existing legal document, with every modification made as a tracked change. Use when the user asks to "change the notice period in clause 9 to 60 days", "add a limitation of liability clause", "soften this indemnity", "delete the exclusivity provision", "fix the wording of this definition", or "redline this agreement so that…". Do NOT use for reviewing a contract against a playbook — use review-contract instead; for explaining the changes in a marked-up draft use summarize-redlines, and for a new agreement from a precedent use draft-new-contract.
- **legal-reasoning** — Reasons over the user's legal documents and answers inline for a lawyer: what a clause says, how provisions interact, and what a contractual stack says after its amendments. Works on contracts and on policies, board minutes and resolutions, term sheets, deeds, leases, court documents, notices and legislation. The neutral fallback when no specialist skill fits; every finding cites its clause. Use when the user asks "what does clause 7 say", "summarize this agreement", "is this term sheet binding", "what was resolved at the board meeting", or "what sits outside our liability cap". Do NOT use to build a key terms table — use compare-key-terms instead; for a memorandum use draft-legal-memo, for a non-legal audience use explain-legal-concepts, and for related contract sets use analyze-related-contracts.
- **review-contract** — Reviews a contract against a supplied playbook: first summarizes a newly introduced playbook for confirmation, then reports each deviation and applies the supported changes as tracked changes. Use when the user asks to "review this contract against our playbook", "check this NDA against our standard positions", "mark up this agreement to our playbook", or "where does this contract deviate from our guidance?". Do NOT use for a single requested edit with no playbook — use edit-contract instead; for building a playbook from precedents use create-playbook.
- **summarize-legal-matters** — Summarizes what happened across named legal matters over a defined period, drawn from the user's Outlook mail, Teams chats and calendar, grouped by matter into developments, decisions, actions, deadlines, open questions and provisional items, with a link or reader-facing reference for every entry. Runs one-off or on a recurring schedule. Use when the user asks to "summarize my matters", "give me a weekly matter update", "what's happened on the Redgrave matter this week", "set up a weekly legal matter report", or "which deadlines are overdue across my matters". Do NOT use to build a chronology from supplied case papers — use build-case-chronology instead; for what changed between document versions use summarize-redlines, and for meeting minutes use draft-minutes-and-resolutions.
- **summarize-redlines** — Summarises the tracked changes in one or more redlined legal documents into a topic-grouped table giving each change, its practical or legal impact, the party that benefits, and a clause locator in the redlined source. Use when the user asks to "summarise the redlines in this contract", "what changed in the other side's markup?", "explain the tracked changes in this version", "who benefits from these amendments?", "give me a summary of the counterparty's edits", or "what did they change since the last draft?". Do NOT use for making or proposing edits — use edit-contract instead; for a document against legislation or policy use identify-regulatory-gaps.

## Install

1. Download the plugin package (the `.zip` on this page).
2. Upload it to your tenant via **M365 admin center › Manage apps › Upload custom app**, or sideload it for testing with the [Microsoft 365 Agents Toolkit CLI](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoftteams/platform/toolkit/microsoft-365-agents-toolkit-cli) (`atk install --file-path <zip> --scope Personal`).
3. Open **Cowork › Sources & Skills › Plugins** and enable it from the **Discover** section.

See [Build plugins for Copilot Cowork](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/copilot/cowork/cowork-plugin-development) for details.
