# Contoso Outdoor Gear: customer journey mapping guide

Fictional sample source for the workshop-pack-builder worked example. Contoso Outdoor Gear is a fictional retailer. All names and numbers are illustrative.

This guide helps Contoso support and store teams map how a customer experiences one task from start to finish, so teams fix the problems customers feel most.

## What a journey map is

A journey map is a picture of one customer goal, told step by step from the customer's point of view. It shows what the customer does, thinks and feels at each step, and which Contoso channel they use. A journey map covers one persona and one goal at a time, for example "return a tent bought online".

A journey map is not a process diagram. A process diagram shows what Contoso does internally. A journey map shows what the customer experiences.

## The five stages

Contoso maps every journey in five stages: Discover, Decide, Buy, Use and Get help. Teams may merge or rename stages when a journey is short, but they keep the customer's goal at the top of the map.

## How to map a journey in a session

1. Pick one persona and one goal.
2. List the steps the customer takes, in order, on sticky notes or a shared whiteboard.
3. For each step, add what the customer does, thinks and feels, and the channel.
4. Mark each step's emotion on a simple scale: positive, neutral or negative.
5. Circle the steps with a negative emotion. These are the pain points.

Contoso recommends groups of four to six people per map, with at least one person who talks to customers every week.

## Finding pain points

A pain point is a step where the customer is confused, waits, repeats information or gives up. Teams find pain points faster when they use real evidence: support tickets, chat transcripts, store notes and survey comments.

Teams should not guess. When the group has no evidence for a step, they mark it as an open question and collect evidence after the session.

## Moments that matter

A moment that matters is a step that strongly shapes whether the customer comes back. Contoso uses two questions to find them: Does this step change how the customer feels about Contoso? Would fixing it change what the customer does next?

## Pilot results

In the 2025 pilot, three Contoso teams mapped the online returns journey. They found 11 pain points. The most common one was customers entering their order number twice. After the teams removed the repeated step, returns-related support contacts fell by 18 percent over eight weeks (Contoso internal sample data, illustrative).

## Common mistakes

- Mapping the internal process instead of the customer's experience.
- Mapping several personas on one map.
- Filling gaps with opinions instead of evidence.
- Ending the session without an owner for each improvement.

Each improvement needs one named owner and a date to check progress.
