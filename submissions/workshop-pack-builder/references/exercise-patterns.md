# Exercise and discussion patterns

Read this in step 4. Pick the pattern that fits what the sources teach and what the objective asks
participants to do. Every pattern works on any topic and in any format. Use a fictional case (Contoso or a
company the user names) for practice data, and label practice numbers as illustrative.

## Contents
1. Required fields for every activity
2. Design rules
3. Hands-on exercise patterns (12)
4. Discussion patterns (6)

## 1. Required fields for every activity

| Field | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `objective` | One sentence: what the activity proves or produces. Ties to a learning objective. |
| `timebox` | 5, 10, 15, 20, 30, 45 or 60 minutes. |
| `instructions` | Three to six numbered steps a participant can follow without the facilitator. |
| `materials` | Every handout, file, tool, board or prop the activity needs. |
| `expected_output` | The artifact or decision each person or group ends with. Countable where possible ("one map with at least eight steps"). |
| `debrief` | Two or three questions for the share-back. |
| `fallback` | What to do when a demo, tool, connection or breakout fails, or the room runs short of time. |
| `sources` | The source refs the content rests on (for example `S2.slide7`). |

## 2. Design rules

- Learners decide before they see the answer: predict, sort or choose first, then reveal.
- Practice data contains planted problems: some items are clean, each other item carries exactly one
  problem that exactly one rule catches, plus one decoy. This keeps the debrief sharp.
- The starting state should fail at least half the checks, and applying the source's recommended
  practice should pass all of them. Test this before the session when the exercise has a scoring rule.
- Every scenario card names one decider: one role, never a committee.
- Share-back is short: one insight per group, not a full report-out.
- Content comes from the sources. When an exercise needs a fact the sources do not hold, mark it as a
  gap or use an obviously fictional practice value labeled illustrative.

## 3. Hands-on exercise patterns

| # | Pattern | How it works | Best for | Participants leave with |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Stress test with scenario cards | Build a plan or policy, then cards throw realistic events at it; each card is caught, partly caught or missed. | Policies, controls, budgets, plans | The tested plan and the decisions with reasons |
| 2 | Replay, step by step | A practice week, month or project plays back one step at a time; each step surfaces an event the plan must handle. | Operating rhythms, processes, escalation paths | The rhythm or routing table and a step log |
| 3 | Calculator with skeptic cards | Fill a value or cost calculation; skeptic cards challenge each assumption. | Business cases, estimates, prioritization | The calculation and the assumptions that survived, with evidence level |
| 4 | Card sort with scoring | Sort items into buckets (now, next, later; keep, change, drop); the sort is scored against the source's rules. | Backlogs, prioritization, classification | The sorted list with a reason per item |
| 5 | Hunt | Inspect a practice artifact and flag hidden problems; reveal what was planted. | Quality, compliance, readiness, review skills | The found-problem list with owners and fixes |
| 6 | Rescue | Rewrite a weak artifact (brief, email, plan, prompt, design) against a rubric from the sources. | Writing, briefing, design quality | The rescued artifact and its rubric score |
| 7 | Decide and see consequences | Make a sequence of choices; each card shows the downstream effect of earlier choices. | Governance, operating models, trade-offs | The decision path and the consequences accepted |
| 8 | Builder | Assemble an artifact from parts (template, checklist, charter); test it against real use cases. | Building templates, plans, playbooks | The finished artifact |
| 9 | Read-aloud cards | The facilitator reads a situation; the room picks one move and names one decider. | Large or virtual groups, short slots | One move and decider per card |
| 10 | Hot seat | Answer requests or tickets in character; check each answer against the source's model answer. | Support, coaching, customer conversations | A routing table or set of model answers |
| 11 | Predict, then reveal | Predict a number or outcome, then see the source's answer; accuracy is scored. | Building intuition about scale, cost, time | Predictions vs actuals and the lessons drawn |
| 12 | Red team the plan | Write a plan, then red team cards attack its weakest assumptions. | Plans, forecasts, launches | The hardened plan and accepted risks with owners |

Also useful for hands-on topics:
- **Worked example, then your turn ("we show, you do"):** demonstrate one round on the fictional case,
  then participants repeat it on their own work. Every field shows the case value and a tip to adapt it.
- **Two paths:** Path A "learn by building" and Path B "fastest" (start from a ready-made file). Either
  path counts. Useful when skill levels in the room differ.
- **Lab kit:** sample data and finished examples bundled as handouts, so participants can finish after
  the session.

## 4. Discussion patterns

| Pattern | How it works | Timebox |
|---|---|---|
| Chat waterfall | Everyone types an answer, waits, then posts at the same time. | 5 |
| Poll and explain | Vote, then two people explain opposite votes. | 5 |
| Think, pair, share | One minute alone, three in pairs, one share per pair. | 5 to 10 |
| One word, one sentence | Each person names one word, then expands one sentence. Good opener and closer. | 5 |
| Fishbowl | A small group discusses while the rest observe, then swap. | 15 to 20 |
| Commitment round | Each person or group posts one next step, one owner and one check date. | 5 to 10 |

## 5. Fallback menu

Pick one per activity and write it into the activity's `fallback`.

| What fails | Fallback |
|---|---|
| Live demo or tool | Show the slide or printed walkthrough of the same steps; participants follow on paper |
| Breakout rooms | Run the activity in the main room with one shared board; rotate who contributes |
| Shared whiteboard | Use the table on the exercise sheet in a shared document |
| Network or access | Use the offline practice data in the participant workbook |
| Time runs short | Each group shares one result in chat; the full debrief moves to the follow-up email |
| Low participation | Call on people by name with an easy first question; switch to pairs |
