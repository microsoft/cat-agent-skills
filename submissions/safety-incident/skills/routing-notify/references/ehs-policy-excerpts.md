# Internal EHS Policy — Excerpts (grounding)

Reference excerpts the retrieval and write-up skills cite when assembling and drafting an incident
packet. These mirror a typical plant EHS management-system policy aligned to OSHA 1904 and ISO
45001. Cited as `ehs-policy-excerpts.md #<section>`.

## 1. Scope & definitions

- **Incident** — any unplanned event that caused, or could have caused, injury, illness, or damage.
- **Near miss** — an incident with no injury/illness or loss, but with the potential for one. Near
  misses are **reported and investigated** as leading indicators (they are not OSHA recordable).
- **Recordable** — a work-related injury/illness meeting the OSHA 1904.7 general recording criteria
  (see `osha-recordability-rules.md`).
- **Reportable** — a fatality, in-patient hospitalization, amputation, or loss of an eye that must
  be reported to OSHA under 1904.39.

## 2. Reporting duty & audit trail

- Every incident and near miss is entered into the EHS system of record. **No incident is deleted;
  reclassification is versioned** with the name of the approver and the reason (audit trail intact).
- A recordable or reportable determination may only be **downgraded** with documented EHS-manager
  sign-off. Field or supervisor pressure to "log it as first aid" does not change the determination.

## 3. Timeliness (internal checklist)

| Section | Item | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Initial report logged | Same shift |
| 3.2 | Supervisor first review | Within 24 hours |
| 3.3 | OSHA 300 entry (if recordable) | Within 7 calendar days of learning it is recordable |
| 3.4 | OSHA report (if reportable) | Within the 1904.39 clock — 8 h (fatality) / 24 h (hospitalization, amputation, loss of eye) |
| 3.5 | Investigation / RCA complete | Per severity: low 5 days · medium 10 days · high/critical 30 days |

## 4. Investigation & RCA

- Every recordable case gets a documented root-cause analysis (5-Why minimum; fishbone for
  high/critical). Corrective actions have owners and due dates and are tracked to closure.
- Repeat incidents on the same asset/area trigger a **systemic** review and a JHA/HIRA
  re-evaluation before the task returns to normal operation.

## 5. Boundaries (what the assistant does not do)

- The assistant **drafts** the classification, routing, notifications and RCA template. It does
  **not** file the OSHA report, send notifications, or close the incident. A human EHS owner reviews
  and signs off — this preserves the reporting checklist and audit trail.
