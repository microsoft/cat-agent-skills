# Slide design

Read this before building a PowerPoint brief. Patterns decide **what each slide
shows**; the template's layouts decide **where it goes**. Plan every slide's
pattern and layout first, then build.

## 1. Inventory the layouts

List the layouts in the open deck or supplied template, and sort them by their
placeholders (names vary, so check placeholder types first):

| Family | Placeholders | Typical names |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cover | Center title + subtitle | Title Slide |
| Statement | Title + short text, or title only, large type | Section Header, Quote |
| Content | Title + one body/object | Title and Content |
| Two column | Title + two bodies, optionally with headings | Two Content, Comparison |
| Picture | Title + picture placeholder | Picture with Caption |
| Canvas | Title only | Title Only |
| Closing | Closing or contact text | Closing, Thank You |

Ignore vertical-text layouts and layouts made for the template's sample content
(agenda grids, team-photo walls). A template is **usable** when it has a cover, a content layout, and at least
one other family, and it carries a real design (its own theme colors, fonts,
or background art). The plain default theme of a new blank presentation
("Office Theme", white with no styling) is not a template. A usable template
means using only its layouts, theme fonts, and theme colors. Otherwise, use
the built-in design (section 6) on the deck's plainest layouts.

## 2. Patterns

Never pad evidence to reach a pattern's minimum; use the slide's fallback.

| ID | Pattern | Build it as | Minimum evidence | Family |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | Mandate cover | Account name as title; subtitle line with purpose, audience, as-of date, source scope | Account, purpose | Cover |
| P2 | Executive summary | Title is the one-sentence takeaway. Below: a 2x3 grid of compact cells (customer priority, our position, best hypothesis, biggest blocker, key unknowns, next move), each with an evidence tag and source marker. Dense: about 150 words | The other slides, drafted last | Content or canvas |
| P3 | KPI tiles | 2 to 4 tiles: big number, unit and period, one-line "so what", source marker | 2 sourced figures | Canvas |
| P4 | Chart and takeaway | Native, editable column, bar, or line chart; takeaway and caveats in the side column | 3 comparable sourced points (same unit, scope, basis) | Two column or content |
| P5 | Priority pillars | 2 to 3 columns: priority, evidence, desired outcome, implication for us | 2 evidenced priorities | Two column, Comparison, or canvas |
| P6 | Stakeholder map | Role cards grouped by function, each with name, role, and a badge: verified, unconfirmed, or conflicting. Connectors only for evidenced relationships | 2 named roles | Canvas |
| P7 | Timeline | Horizontal line with dated markers and short labels; past to future | 3 dated events | Content or canvas |
| P8 | Side by side | Status quo or incumbent against the alternative, with evidenced attributes; write "unknown" rather than leaving gaps | 2 options | Two column or Comparison |
| P9 | Hypothesis cards | 2 to 3 cards: need, evidence, hypothesis, validating question, confidence tag | 1 evidenced need | Canvas |
| P10 | Risk and gap register | Color-coded rows or a compact table: risk or gap, evidence, impact, mitigation | 2 risks or gaps | Content |
| P11 | Closing: next engagements | Lightweight, about 40 words. Up to 4 known customer engagements (date, meeting, our owner). If none are known, show 2 to 4 engagement steps marked proposed or unassigned. End with a one-line success signal | 1 engagement or step | Closing, else content |
| P12 | Source register | Compact table: marker, source, date or period, locator | Always | Content |

Default plan:

| Slide | Primary | Fallback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Mandate | P1 | none |
| 2 Executive summary | P2 | Two column: takeaway left, 5 to 6 tagged one-line points right |
| 3 Business and performance | P4 | P3, then a two-column profile |
| 4 Priorities and why now | P5 | P7 when triggers are dated |
| 5 Stakeholders | P6 | Two column: verified roles, and unknowns |
| 6 Relationship and footprint | P7 | P3 of footprint facts |
| 7 Alternatives | P8 | Compact table |
| 8 Opportunity hypotheses | P9 | 2x2 grid, only when both axes are evidenced |
| 9 Buying path, risks, gaps | P10 | Process sketch when buying steps are evidenced |
| 10 Closing | P11 | none |
| Appendix | P12 | none |

## 3. Variety rules

Check the plan before building, and the finished deck before delivery:

- **Layouts.** Use at least 4 different layouts in a 10-slide brief, and never
  the same layout on more than 2 consecutive slides. If the plan breaks this,
  move the middle slide to its pattern's other family.
- **Tables.** At most one third of main slides, rounded down (3 of 10), and
  never two in a row. The source register does not count.
- **Charts.** When any section has 3 comparable sourced points, include at
  least one native chart. Never chart estimates, mixed units, or targets
  plotted as actuals. Label the axis unit and period, and put the source marker
  under the chart.
- **One dominant object** per slide, plus at most one short text block. About
  60 words of body text, except P2.
- **Titles** state the supported takeaway; otherwise use an honest question.
- **Evidence tags.** Use one consistent marker across the deck: a filled chip
  for facts, an outlined chip for hypotheses, and a dashed chip for unknowns,
  in theme accent colors with the word visible (color alone is not enough).
- **Fit.** Body text 12 pt or larger, labels and sources 10 pt or larger. If
  content does not fit, split or shorten it; never shrink below these sizes or
  overflow.
- **Fill layouts properly.** Put titles and body text in the layout's
  placeholders, not in loose text boxes over them. Delete any placeholder left
  empty. Align drawn shapes to the layout's content area and to each other.

## 4. Text formatting

Make one key number or phrase per slide bold or in an accent color. Use short
parallel phrases rather than paragraphs; keep a bullet to one line where
possible. Put source markers in a consistent small style (for example
`[NS-1 §2]`). Avoid all caps for body text, underlines that are not links,
and more than two text sizes in one block.

## 5. Images

Use an image only when it is real and relevant: images supplied in the sources
or template (for example, a customer logo in the source packet), built-in icons
when the host provides them, and the charts and diagrams you draw. Never
generate photos or illustrations of real people, offices, or products and
present them as genuine, and never fetch or recreate a logo the sources did not
supply. Choose a picture layout only when a qualifying image exists; never leave
a picture placeholder empty.

## 6. Built-in design (no usable template)

Use this only when the template is not usable. 16:9 slides, 0.5-inch margins, a
title band at the top, and a source footer at the bottom. Aptos or Calibri;
titles 28 to 32 pt, body 14 to 16 pt, KPI numbers 40 to 54 pt, labels 10 to
11 pt. Off-white background and near-black text, one accent color for
emphasis, and three muted tones reserved for fact, hypothesis, and unknown
tags. Flat fills with 1-point rules and no shadows; square corners except pill
status chips; no clip art. Build the same patterns with these tokens.
