# Account brief content map

Use this as an adaptable coverage map, not a required count or fixed layout.
Standard depth is approximately ten main slides plus a source appendix. A quick
brief combines topics into roughly five main slides; a deeper brief expands only
where the decision and evidence justify it. No agenda or divider slides needed
by default. Do not invent content to fill an empty section.

| Position | Topic and content | Pattern (see [slide design](slide-design.md)) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Mandate.** Account identity, audience, purpose/decision, seller perspective when known, as-of date, source scope. | P1 cover |
| 2 | **Executive takeaways.** Main customer priority, current position, most useful opportunity hypothesis, biggest blocker, recommended next move. Draft last. | P2 executive summary |
| 3 | **Business and performance.** Business model, relevant units/geographies, market, financial/operating signals with period/unit, recent developments and their relevance. | P4 chart and takeaway; else P3 KPI tiles |
| 4 | **Priorities and why now.** Customer strategy, initiatives, target outcomes/KPIs, triggers, timing, constraints, and implications for discovery. | P5 priority pillars; else P7 timeline |
| 5 | **Stakeholders and decision dynamics.** Verified roles, remit, interests, confirmed decision roles, relationship owners, and access gaps. | P6 stakeholder map; else two-column lists |
| 6 | **Relationship and footprint.** Known history, deployed services/products, outcomes, contract/renewal facts, service problems, partners. A new prospect may have no confirmed relationship. | P7 timeline; else P3 KPI tiles |
| 7 | **Incumbents and alternatives.** Known suppliers, status quo, build-in-house, switching constraints, ecosystem, and supported seller differentiation. | P8 side by side; else compact table |
| 8 | **Opportunity hypotheses.** Customer need and evidence, possible use case/value, seller fit when supported, dependencies, next validation question. | P9 hypothesis cards; else evidenced 2x2 |
| 9 | **Buying path, risks, gaps.** Confirmed decision/procurement process, budget status, timing, security/regulatory concerns when applicable, relationship/qualification gaps, contradictions, mitigations. | P10 risk and gap register |
| 10 | **Closing: next engagements.** Lightweight; not the focus of the brief. Known upcoming customer engagements (date, meeting, our owner); if none are known, the engagement plan as 2 to 4 proposed or unassigned steps; one-line success signal. | P11 closing: next engagements |
| Appendix | **Sources and unresolved questions.** Claim-linked sources with titles/publishers, dates/periods, locators, access date; material unknowns and validation steps. | P12 source register |

## Composition choices

- For PowerPoint, plan each slide's pattern and template layout with
  [slide design](slide-design.md) before building. Vary layouts; tables are
  capped at one third of main slides, and a chart is expected whenever three
  comparable sourced figures exist. Do not make every slide a table or bullets.
- Do not treat a role list as an influence map. A reporting relationship, decision
  role, or supporter/blocker label needs its own evidence.
- Customer-market competitors belong in business context; competing solution
  vendors/status quo belong in alternatives. Do not merge the two taxonomies.
- For a new prospect, replace retention/renewal discussion with access and
  discovery. For an existing customer, surface relationship/service problems
  before expansion. Do not suppress bad news to strengthen the opportunity story.
- A topic can be combined or omitted when immaterial, but a material unknown must
  remain visible. Missing budget is not zero budget; no observed relationship is
  not proof there is none.
- Use an assertion title only when supported. Otherwise an honest question or
  descriptive title is better than a manufactured conclusion.

## Deck conventions

Use slides, editable text and tables, compact citation markers, and a source
appendix. Keep material caveats and sources on visible slides.

If space is tight, combine business/priorities or relationship/alternatives.
Avoid squeezing relationship and opportunity matrices into unreadable pages.
Evidence limits and the decision matter more than a target slide count.
