# Anchor library

Concrete reference values for step 3 of the workflow. Pick the **closest familiar**
anchor, not the most impressive one — an anchor the audience doesn't recognize is
worse than no anchor.

All figures are approximate and rounded for memorability. **Always present them with
"≈", "about," or "roughly"** (see Accuracy rules in SKILL.md). These are slow-moving
reference points, chosen because they drift by a few percent a year rather than
overnight; anything volatile (market sizes, competitor revenue, stock prices) is
deliberately excluded — never source those from memory.

**But populations do drift.** The figures here were last reviewed **2026-07**.
National counts creep — Canada passed 40M in 2023 — so if a comparison is
load-bearing for the argument rather than just colour, sanity-check it against a
current source before it goes on a slide. Landmark heights, distances, area, and
data-volume conversions are stable and need no re-checking.

## 3. Rate over time

Best for: turning an annual or cumulative total into a felt cadence. This is the
cheapest anchor to compute and often the most visceral.

| Annual total | Roughly |
|--------------|---------|
| 365 / year | one a day |
| ~8,760 / year | one an hour |
| ~500K / year | one a minute |
| ~2M / year | one every 16 seconds |
| ~10M / year | three a second |
| ~31.5M / year | one a second |

**Formula:** seconds between events = 31,536,000 ÷ annual total. Round to something
sayable ("every 17 seconds," not "every 16.6 seconds"). The input must be a *flow* —
new users per year, orders per year. A stock metric like monthly active users is a
population, not a rate; converting MAU to "users per second" states something the
data never said.

Container framings also work: "enough to fill a stadium every week," "a new team the
size of ours every quarter."

## 4. Money equivalent

Best for: making an abstract cost, saving, or valuation tangible.

Reliable framings that need no external data:
- **Per unit of time** — "$4.2M/year ≈ $11,500 a day."
- **Per person** — divide by headcount, customers, or citizens: "≈ $340 per employee."
- **As a share** — "≈ 12% of the budget," "≈ 3 weeks of revenue."
- **As a recurring purchase** — "≈ the price of a coffee per user per month."

**Do not** state a competitor's revenue, a market size, or a valuation from memory.
Use a labeled placeholder — see Accuracy rules.

## 5. Competitive / benchmark

Best for: "we're ahead" or "we're behind." Usually the most persuasive anchor for a
business audience. Visualize with a bar or column chart.

Safe benchmarks you can compute from the deck itself:
- last year / last quarter (same metric, earlier period)
- the target or plan number
- the industry average **if it appears elsewhere in the user's deck**
- the sum of the other segments in the same data

Anything else — a named rival's figure, an analyst market size — is external. Insert
`[Competitor B — confirm figure]` and let the user fill it in. **Never invent one.**

## 6. Data volume

Best for: storage, throughput, logs, model training sets.

| Amount | Roughly |
|--------|---------|
| 1 GB | ~250 songs · ~1 hour of HD video |
| 1 TB | ~250,000 photos · ~500 hours of HD video |
| 1 PB | ~2,000 years of continuous music |
| all printed books ever | ~50 PB |

## 7. Analogy and metaphor bank

Use when no numeric anchor fits. Map the abstract shape of the idea to a concrete
image, then **show the image**.

| Shape of the idea | Metaphor |
|-------------------|----------|
| progressive loss at each stage | a funnel, a leaky bucket |
| a sequence with handoffs | a relay race, an assembly line |
| a small visible part, a large hidden one | an iceberg, a root system |
| one blocker throttling everything | a bottleneck, a single-lane bridge |
| compounding growth | a snowball, compound interest |
| many small parts making a whole | a mosaic, a flywheel |
| two forces in tension | a seesaw, a tug of war |
| a threshold that changes everything | a tipping point, water boiling |

## Choosing between anchors

| If the point is… | Reach for |
|------------------|-----------|
| "this is bigger than you think" | population · physical size |
| "we're winning / losing" | competitive benchmark |
| "this is relentless" | rate over time |
| "this is expensive / cheap" | money equivalent |
| "this is the mechanism" | analogy |

**One anchor per slide.** Stacking two comparisons halves the impact of both.
