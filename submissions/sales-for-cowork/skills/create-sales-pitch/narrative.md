# Customer Pitch Deck narrative

Build an audience-specific commercial argument from supplied evidence. Reading the complete source
does not require displaying every detail. Each page answers
a useful buyer question; its layout, design treatment, and reference choice must
make that answer visible.

## Audience and stage adaptations

These adapt the same customer product/solution pitch, not separate workflows.

- **Cold first meeting:** plant a relevant idea, establish enough credibility,
  and earn a second meeting with a decision maker or marketing buyer.
- **Warm-lead product introduction:** explain what the product does, why it
  matters to this business, and what the champion can do next.
- **Industry-specific pitch:** show understanding of the vertical's operating
  context before connecting the platform to its needs. Never invent industry
  facts that were not supplied or researched through an approved source.
- **Internal enablement:** standardize the external story for a new seller or
  channel partner, including claims, proof boundaries, and the intended CTA.

## Default storytelling logic

1. **External world:** frame the relevant market, industry, or operating reality.
2. **Gap or opportunity:** identify what the audience is missing, losing, or
   unable to do today.
3. **Product as the answer:** name the relevant answer to that gap, not its catalog.
4. **Seller context and why-us:** identify the provider and relevant business
   context, then connect customer need → specific capability → bounded proof →
   reason to choose or evaluate this seller before enumerating solutions.
   Unrelated corporate history, scale alone or unsupported superiority is not
   why-us. Distinguish the seller from the customer and any historical case.
5. **Value and proof:** show bounded ROI potential, benchmark evidence, or social
   proof without overstating causality.
6. **Call to action:** ask for one appropriate next commitment.

These are reasoning stages, not six required slides. Combine, omit, or reorder
them for the actual conversation. Do not invent numbers, history, endorsements,
or contact information to populate an outline.

## Adaptive default sequence

Use this as a planning scaffold when the user has not supplied a better order.
When the user omits a page count, default to exactly 18 slides. Concise means
focused content, not a shorter default. Do not ask for a page count solely because
it is omitted. Explicit user counts, ranges and order override this default;
choose a total within a requested range, not necessarily 18. The total includes
cover, Agenda, section dividers, company introduction, why-us, proof, CTA and close.
Adapt page roles to the buyer and evidence while keeping the resolved total. Reserve
seller introduction and earned why-us separately from navigation and case detail
in the first-meeting content budget; do not spend their space on extra inventory.
An explicitly shorter count can combine background with the answer, why-us with proof, and CTA with
close; omit standalone dividers when the count/order or explicit opt-out requires
it, using compact section cues where useful. A longer deck earns space with
relevant seller context, distinct evidence, limitations and decision explanations,
not repeated claims, empty dividers or one KPI split across filler pages. Keep the
story's logical order even when page count changes. Include a visible Agenda by
default: two or three distinct audience-relevant questions or topics, not
"Introduction / Overview / Conclusion" filler. Keep its left title and right
vertical items through normal compression unless the user explicitly opts out
or specifies an incompatible count/order.

Keep a full-image cover and a recognizable Thank You/relationship close.
A CTA may share the closing page, but an ask or
missing-evidence checklist alone is not a relationship close. Use a brief thank
you or invitation to continue and only supplied contact details. Explicit user
media/style restrictions override these defaults, not template availability.

The 18-slide outline below uses three sections: opportunity (4–7), provider fit
(8–12), and evidence / decision (13–17). Adapt their labels to the buyer and use
the same labels on the Agenda and dividers. Each content page must answer a distinct
buyer question. If a planned proof or capability is unsupported, replace that role
with a useful, explicitly proposed validation question, decision criterion or
limitation; do not invent evidence or pad with repeated claims or empty slides.
If the source cannot support a meaningful plan, disclose the grounding limitation.

In a multi-section pitch, place meaningful dividers at real narrative transitions,
not before every slide. Match each divider's label/number to its Agenda item and
the content that follows. Small repeated section cues may sustain orientation
within a section; a title appearing only in notes is not a divider.

## Adapt to the conversation

- **Cold first meeting:** establish relevant seller identity and context early:
  name who we are, whom we help and the relevant work supported by the source.
  Before solution enumeration, compose a visible synthesis using
  **buyer need / our relevant capability / source proof & limits / what it enables**.
  Align each need with its capability and evidence, then state the bounded value
  or test it motivates. Turn unknowns into questions to validate, not fabricated
  superiority. An ecosystem inventory, capability list or isolated case statistic
  does not make this connection for the buyer. For an explicitly shorter count, combine these
  beats on one page; keep the identity and complete argument visible.
- **Warm lead:** spend less time establishing the category and more time mapping
  product capabilities to the lead's stated business context. Compress already
  known seller background; retain the reason to choose this answer.
- **Industry-specific pitch:** adapt examples, terminology, and proof to supplied
  vertical evidence; do not use generic industry decoration as a substitute.
- **Internal enablement:** make the standard story, proof boundaries, objection
  handling, and CTA reusable by sellers or partners.
- **Explicit custom brief:** honor assigned count, order, emphasis, brand, and
  media constraints. User direction wins over this scaffold.
- **Approved content library:** select current relevant material, remove
  repetition, and customize with minimal transitions; do not reinterpret facts.

Live delivery privileges one visible claim with concise speech support. Reading
delivery needs enough local context. Depth is not smaller text.

## Choose and adapt references

Read `reference/CATALOG.md`, then only the selected infographic contract or
icon manifest entries. Every reference is a relationship model, not a locked
template.

- `timeline.icon-phases` suits company history and milestone sequencing. Add
  concise explanation, recolor to the active theme, or substitute semantically
  better icons while preserving round icon nodes and the continuous timeline.
- `process.three-stations` suits grouped operating stages, not dated chronology.
- `comparison.criteria-matrix` suits fair alternatives under shared criteria.
- `list.capability-grid` suits distinct capabilities mapped to customer tasks;
  it does not imply sequence, chronology or causal growth.
- `before-after.paired-state-rows` pairs the same concern across two states, not
  steps or unrelated benefits.
- `allocation.partition-ring` requires an exhaustive, mutually exclusive,
  additive whole; an overlap study must be partitioned before drawing sectors.
- Curated icons support categories, capabilities, actions, industries, and CTA
  cues. They do not establish a metric, endorsement, status, or capability.

Allowed adaptations include theme-aware recolor, replacing placeholder copy and
data, adding bounded description, reflowing labels, and scaling the complete
component to fit. Do not remove defining icons, change circular nodes into
unrelated geometry, break arrows or topology, distort aspect ratio, or dissolve
a relationship into generic equal cards.

## Evidence review

Preserve supplied, computed, assumed, proposed, and missing states. Missing
information differs from an observed negative result. Carry units, dates,
populations, denominators, and limitations with the claim, not only in notes.
Qualify the headline
or body that carries it; a footer or notes cannot negate a categorical assertion.
Preserve the literal "Not tested" and "Not provided" states when they appear;
neither is evidence of a negative capability finding.
Conversely, explicit non-support remains non-support, not merely an evaluation or pilot exclusion.
Compression must retain material stop/veto conditions and the actions they block, not just name
the related test. Preserve prerequisites and decision authority in the CTA. Do not assign an
unspecified role to a known stakeholder; keep it unassigned or explicitly proposed for confirmation.
Independent samples are not matched panels; percentage points are not growth.
Aggregate medians do not prove each task's saving or the median paired saving.
First-draft observations do not establish total delivery effort, causation,
realized profit, or universal ROI.

Keep the source's comparator and period attached to the measured population;
do not transfer a comparison or survey base to neighboring metrics. Devices,
shipments, searches and people are different units, not additive or deduplicated
incremental addressable audiences. Complementarity can motivate a hypothesis,
not prove incremental reach. A planned average spend for eligible respondents
is neither actual spend nor revenue per customer. An index with an unspecified
base stays unspecified or is omitted. Category or survey fit is an inference
to validate, not observed customer usage, task-hours or a verified feature.

Separate ecosystem presence from purchasable inventory or customer product fit.
Carry app/listing availability, eligibility and other prerequisites into every
recommendation and CTA that depends on them; an example distribution journey is
not a guaranteed placement or recommendation. Historical case growth is reported
observation, not causal lift or a forecast. Keep case identity, strategy mix,
measurement definition and missing attribution/period visible; do not merge
results from different cases or relabel a strategy mix as budget allocation.
An illustrative example with unspecified identity stays unspecified, not the
same or a different customer by inference.
Supplied simulation material is not independently verified evidence. Never invent
customer capabilities, internal problems, current partnerships or missing study
methods to make the why-us argument stronger.

Read final cells, chart caches/workbooks, and visible labels against the source.
Keep material precision, source scope, and human review visible. Tie each release
or spending condition to its action. Omit or bound unsupported claims rather
than making them more certain in notes.
