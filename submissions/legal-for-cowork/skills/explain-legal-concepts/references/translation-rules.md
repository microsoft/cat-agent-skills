# Translation fidelity rules, audience registers and the evidence standard

Loaded on demand by `explain-legal-concepts`. Nothing here overrides the guardrails in SKILL.md.

## Fidelity rules

- **Lead with the consequence.** Say what happens, to whom, and by when, then cite the clause. "We
  have to give them 90 days' written notice before we can walk away" is clearer than "Clause 12.2
  governs termination for convenience."
- **Keep every number exact.** Copy amounts, percentages, caps, dates, durations, notice periods,
  volume commitments and thresholds precisely. Rounding is a defect, not simplification.
- **Preserve the strength of every obligation.** `Must` is not `should`; `may` is not `will`;
  `reasonable endeavours` is not a guarantee. Preserve language introduced by `subject to`,
  `provided that` and similar conditions.
- **Attribute every obligation.** Say who owes what to whom. Never leave a non-legal reader with
  "notice must be given".
- **Keep conditions and carve-outs.** An obligation without its exception is a different obligation.
  State when — and only when — a right applies, in the same sentence.
- **Retire legalese but retain useful labels.** Replace Latin, doublets and archaisms with everyday
  language. Where the reader will meet a defined term again, explain it and keep the label once in
  brackets with its clause.
- **Say when the documents do not answer.** Silence is a finding, never evidence of a market
  position, and never a licence to infer a missing term from a document that was not supplied.

## Perspective

Establish which party the reader is on from the request or the documents and translate from that
side: "we have to give 90 days' notice" is actionable where "the Reseller shall give notice" is not.
Where the side cannot be established, name the parties instead of saying "we", give every obligation
an owner, and say why the explanation is neutral. Never guess the user's side, and never replace the
neutral default with a guessed party. Audience register is not perspective: "for the sales team"
tells you how to pitch the language, not which contracting party the reader represents.

## Audience registers

- **Minimal legal experience:** avoid legalese as far as possible. Everyday words, short sentences,
  active voice, one idea per sentence. Explain every unavoidable legal or defined term immediately
  in practical language; never assume the reader knows what it means.
- **Some legal familiarity:** prefer plain English, but retain useful legal labels after explaining
  their practical effect.
- **Legally experienced:** use concise legal terminology where it improves precision, but still lead
  with the practical consequence and avoid archaic or needlessly dense drafting. A legally
  experienced *audience for a plain English explanation* is still not a request for legal analysis —
  that is `legal-reasoning`.
- **Custom:** follow the user's stated level and explanation needs without relaxing any fidelity
  rule.

## Evidence standard

- Every explanation point carries a document and a location. Where a point cannot be located, remove
  it or mark it `Check with Legal` and say why.
- Quotes are exact and contiguous. Never approximate wording from a partially readable source.
- Never fabricate or reconstruct a clause number, party, date, figure, defined term or document
  title.
- Preserve differences across agreements. Never flatten a portfolio into "broadly similar terms".
- Keep unreadable documents in the coverage note and state the effect on the answer.

## Chat citation protocol

1. Finish inspection, then plan the complete answer and citation set before writing.
2. Quote the exact operative wording for every grounded statement.
3. Attribute each quote inline to the exact filename plus its clause, section, schedule, exhibit,
   page, or sheet and cell range.
4. Write the explanation as the normal final reply, each attribution immediately after the
   punctuation of the sentence it grounds. Never invent a locator: where one cannot be established,
   quote the wording and say so. Search result records carry no clause locator — attribute those
   sources in human-readable prose instead.
