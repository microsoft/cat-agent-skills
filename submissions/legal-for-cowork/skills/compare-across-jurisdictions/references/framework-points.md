# Frozen framework — point floors by mode

Loaded on demand by `compare-across-jurisdictions` at Step 3.2. These lists are a
**starting floor, not a closed menu, and each point applies only as applicable**:
add any point the question requires, and omit any listed point the question cannot
engage — recording the omission and its reason in Question and scope. Do both while
framing, before research begins, because the framework is frozen once retrieval
starts. After the freeze, Step 3.3 applies without exception: no point is deleted,
however thin. Omitting an inapplicable point at framing time is not deletion; it is
what keeps a narrow question from carrying `Not researched` cells it never needed.

## Requirements comparison

Use for statutory or regulatory entitlements, thresholds, notice, eligibility,
filing duties and penalties. Cover, as applicable:

1. Legal basis — the instrument that creates the requirement.
2. Scope — who and what it applies to.
3. Thresholds or eligibility — headcount, turnover, tenure, sector, value.
4. Obligation or entitlement — what must be done or may be claimed.
5. Timing and duration — accrual, notice periods, deadlines, maximum durations.
6. Exceptions, carve-outs and derogations.
7. Extraterritorial reach and anti-waiver rules.
8. Enforcement — regulator, process, private right of action.
9. Penalties — amounts, caps, criminal exposure.
10. In force status — commencement, pending amendment, transitional provisions.

## Litigation treatment

Use for how a point is litigated. Cover, as applicable:

1. Source and controlling authority — statute, code, leading decision.
2. Test or elements the claimant must establish.
3. Burdens and standards of proof, including any shifting burden.
4. Decision-maker — court, tribunal, jury, regulator; specialist lists.
5. Pleading and procedure — pre-action steps, form of claim, interim relief.
6. Evidence and disclosure — scope, privilege, expert evidence.
7. Remedies — declaratory, injunctive, damages, restitution, statutory awards.
8. Limitation — period, trigger, suspension, extension.
9. Appeal — route, permission, standard of review.
10. Defences and bars.

## Splitting compound issues

If a proposed point bundles two questions that could diverge independently — for
example "notice period and payment in lieu" — split it into two rows. A bundled row
produces a cell that is partly `Stated` and partly `Not located`, which the single
cell state cannot express honestly.

## Adding a point after research has begun

Permitted only if you then research it in **every** jurisdiction, or mark it
`Not researched` in every jurisdiction and explain why in Limitations. A point
answered for some jurisdictions and quietly dropped for others breaks the
denominator and makes the coverage counts misleading.
