# Instruments that are not contracts — the detail behind Step 3

The same reasoning discipline applies to any legal instrument; only the
structure changes. Adjust what you look for.

## Establish status first

Executed, draft, or unsigned? Dated? Which version? A conformed or certified
copy? An unsigned draft is not an agreement, and saying so is a finding, not a
caveat. Where a document has no clause numbering, cite by heading, paragraph
number, page, or a short quoted phrase.

## Term sheets, heads of terms, LOIs and MOUs

Identify which provisions are **binding** — typically confidentiality,
exclusivity, costs, governing law — and which are indicative. That distinction
is usually the question being asked. Report the binding/non-binding statement by
its own provision, and note any expiry of exclusivity or of the term sheet
itself.

## Policies, codes and procedures

Who they bind, effective date, owner, review cycle, and whether a given
provision is mandatory or advisory. Note where a policy incorporates another
policy or a law, and whether breach carries a stated consequence.

## Corporate records

- **Minutes and resolutions:** date, attendees, quorum, chair, and what was
  actually *resolved* as against discussed or noted. Distinguish a resolution
  passed from a matter deferred, and note any declared interest or abstention.
- **Constitutional documents:** which article governs, and whether a
  shareholders' agreement overrides it.
- Where the user wants minutes or resolutions **drafted**, that is
  `draft-minutes-and-resolutions`, not this skill.

## Deeds, leases and licences

Parties and their capacity, date of execution and delivery, whether the
instrument is expressed as a deed and executed as one, term and break rights,
rent or fee mechanics and review, repair, alienation and consent provisions, and
the notice machinery. Report execution formalities as observations — whether
they are satisfied as a matter of law is out of scope.

## Court and tribunal documents

Parties and their roles, court, date, procedural posture, and the relief sought.
In a judgment, separate the holding from obiter and note the level of the court.
In a pleading, distinguish what is asserted from what is admitted. Never predict
an outcome.

## Legislation, regulations and guidance

Version and commencement, whether a provision is actually in force, and whether
guidance is binding or advisory. Cite instrument, section or article, and the
version relied on. Reporting what the instrument requires is in scope;
concluding that a document complies with it is not — that is
`identify-regulatory-gaps`.

## Correspondence and notices

Sender, recipient, date, whether marked without prejudice or privileged, and
whether it purports to be a formal notice under an agreement — and if so,
whether it meets that agreement's notice requirements as to form, address,
method and timing. Report the mismatch as a finding; do not conclude the notice
is invalid.

## The boundary, restated for these instruments

- **In scope:** what a provision says, how the mechanism works, what triggers
  it, what qualifies it, how two provisions interact, what the document does not
  address, how instruments differ, and what a named statute or standard
  requires.
- **Out of scope:** whether a clause is enforceable, whether a party is in
  breach, whether the document complies with a regulation, how a court would
  construe it, what the user should do, and risk ratings or scores.
- **General legal concepts** ("what is a fiduciary duty?") are answered from
  general knowledge, without dressing them up as findings about the document or
  attaching document citations. But "how does the time is of the essence clause
  work in *this* contract?" is a document question — reason it from the
  document. For plain English explanation aimed at a non-legal audience, hand
  over to `explain-legal-concepts`.
- That you provide legal reasoning grounded in the documents rather than legal
  advice is a calibration for you, not a caption for your answers. Let it shape
  what you claim; do not print it.
