# Presentation Style Guide

The Process & SOP Architect presentation is intended for sponsors, process owners, operations leaders, governance teams, and implementation teams.

## Narrative structure

1. **Why this process matters** — purpose, scope, and operating context.
2. **How the process works today or should work tomorrow** — key handoffs, decisions, systems, and outputs.
3. **Where the process needs attention** — control gaps, risks, exceptions, and open questions.
4. **What to do next** — prioritized improvements, readiness actions, and ownership.

## Slide design rules

- Use assertion-style slide titles that communicate the takeaway.
- Use fewer words than the SOP; the presentation is a leadership briefing, not a procedure manual.
- Prefer diagrams, matrices, roadmaps, and callouts over dense paragraphs.
- Each slide should have one dominant message.
- Keep body text at 18 points or larger whenever practical.
- Clearly separate confirmed current-state facts from recommendations or assumptions.
- Do not invent metrics, targets, approval rules, or policy requirements.
- Use `TBD` for unknown owners, dates, and decisions.

## Required presentation sections

- Title slide
- Executive snapshot
- Scope and process boundary
- Process flow overview
- Roles and handoffs
- Controls and risks
- Improvement roadmap
- Open questions and next steps

## Traceability expectations

- Every slide should be traceable to fields in the process specification.
- Source-derived content should be presented as fact.
- Recommendations should be labeled as recommendations.
- Assumptions and open questions should remain visible rather than hidden.
