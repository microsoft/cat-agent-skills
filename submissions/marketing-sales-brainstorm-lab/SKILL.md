description: >-
  Use this skill whenever the user wants to brainstorm marketing campaign
  ideas, sales angles, positioning, or a new customer segment, and wants
  genuinely varied ideas grounded in real market or competitor research
  rather than a single polished suggestion.
---

Research first, diverge into genuinely different directions, then converge on
the strongest few with honest tradeoffs.

## Instructions

1. Clarify the brief before ideating: the actual goal (launch, enter a market,
   fix a specific metric, generate campaign concepts, find a new segment), the
   audience, hard constraints (budget, timeline, channels, brand voice), and
   what a good outcome looks like. Batch related questions rather than asking
   one at a time.

2. Research before generating ideas. When web access is available, look up
   relevant context: how competitors are currently positioning themselves,
   recent campaigns in the space, audience pain points, and any credible
   market data. Cite sources. When web access isn't available, ask the user
   for what they already know (named competitors, recent campaigns, customer
   feedback) instead of inventing market facts to fill the gap.

3. Diverge before converging. Generate a genuinely wide spread of ideas using
   a structured technique rather than three variations of the same concept:
   - **How might we**: reframe the goal as several distinct "how might we"
     questions, then generate ideas per reframe.
   - **Audience x channel matrix**: cross different audience segments against
     different channels to surface combinations not yet considered.
   - **SCAMPER**: substitute, combine, adapt, modify, put to another use,
     eliminate, reverse, applied to an existing campaign or offer.
   - **Problem reversal**: brainstorm the worst possible version of the
     campaign, then invert each bad idea into a genuinely different good one.
   See `references/ideation-techniques.md` for the full method on each.

4. Converge deliberately. Score or cluster the generated ideas against the
   stated goal and constraints, and present the top few with a one-line
   rationale each. State the real tradeoff for each: reach versus cost, risk
   versus novelty, brand-safe versus attention-grabbing. Don't silently filter
   down to only the safest ideas; if a bold idea was generated, present it
   with its actual risk named, and let the user decide.

5. For sales-specific requests, shift the ideation target: angles,
   differentiators, urgency triggers, proof points, and objection handling
   framings rather than campaign concepts.

6. Once the user picks a direction, offer to develop it further (a fuller
   brief, a campaign outline, specific copy) rather than assuming they want
   that automatically.

7. If the next step is producing actual outreach copy, ad copy, or a
   structured competitive comparison, say so and point to the skill built for
   that rather than reproducing its format here.

## Guardrails

- Label every unverified market claim, competitor detail, or statistic as an
  assumption to confirm. Never present it as fact.
- The divergence step must produce genuinely different directions, not
  near-duplicate wording of the same idea. If every generated idea targets the
  same audience and channel, that's a sign to redo the divergence step with a
  different technique.
- Never lift a real competitor's actual campaign concept wholesale. Reference
  and adapt the underlying idea, don't reproduce their execution.
- Don't skip the research step just because ideation feels faster without it.
  Ideas grounded in a real gap or a real audience pain point outperform
  ideas generated from the brief alone.

## Tone

Energetic but grounded. Present ideas with genuine variety and real
tradeoffs, not a sales pitch for each one.
