# Ideation techniques

Four structured techniques for generating genuinely different ideas instead of
near-duplicates of the first idea that comes to mind. Pick whichever fits the
brief, or run more than one and compare what each surfaces.

## How might we

Turn the goal into several distinct reframes, each starting with "How might
we...". A single goal usually supports at least four or five different
reframes; generate ideas separately under each rather than blending them.

Example goal: "Grow signups for a budgeting app among young professionals."
- How might we make budgeting feel like a game instead of a chore?
- How might we reach people at the exact moment they overspend?
- How might we get existing users to bring a friend?
- How might we make the first five minutes prove the app's value instantly?

## Audience x channel matrix

List distinct audience segments down one axis and available channels across
the other. Force at least one idea into every cell, including the
combinations that feel unlikely. The unlikely cells are usually where the
genuinely novel ideas show up, since nobody's tried them yet.

## SCAMPER

Applied to an existing campaign, product, or offer:

- **Substitute**: what element could be swapped for something unexpected?
- **Combine**: what could this be merged with (another product, a partner, a
  cultural moment)?
- **Adapt**: what's worked in a completely different industry that could
  transfer here?
- **Modify/magnify**: what if the boldest element were made much bigger, or
  much smaller?
- **Put to another use**: who else, beyond the current target audience, could
  this serve?
- **Eliminate**: what's the version of this with the single most expensive or
  complex element removed?
- **Reverse**: what's the exact opposite of the current approach?

## Problem reversal

Brainstorm the worst possible version of the campaign on purpose (the most
annoying, the most forgettable, the most tone-deaf). Then take each bad idea
and invert it into something genuinely good. This works because it's often
easier to generate a long list of bad ideas fast, and inversion turns that
volume into real variety instead of three safe, similar-sounding concepts.
