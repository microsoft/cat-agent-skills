# Greenwashing Legal and Regulatory Research

**Research date:** 2026-07
**Scope:** Canada, the European Union, and the United Kingdom
**Purpose:** A practical compliance baseline for evaluating public environmental claims. This is not legal advice.

## Jurisdiction codes

Every issue in this document uses one of these codes:

| Code | Region | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `ANY` | Any jurisdiction | A vocabulary or general misleading-claim risk that should be checked everywhere. `ANY` is a screening rule, not a statement that every jurisdiction has an identical statute. |
| `CA` | Canada | A potential violation or compliance issue under the Canadian Competition Act or Competition Bureau guidance. |
| `EU` | European Union | A potential violation or compliance issue under EU consumer law, including Directive (EU) 2024/825 and Member State implementing law. |
| `UK` | United Kingdom | A potential violation or compliance issue under UK consumer law or CMA guidance. |

One claim can produce multiple findings. For example, `eco-friendly` can be an `ANY` vocabulary risk, an `EU` prohibited generic claim from September 27, 2026, and a `CA` or `UK` misleading-claim concern depending on its substantiation and overall impression.

## Working definition

There is no single worldwide statutory definition of greenwashing. Across the reviewed regimes, the common concept is:

> A public environmental representation is greenwashing when its words, imagery, omissions, scope, or supporting evidence create a materially misleading impression that a product, service, brand, business, or activity has a better environmental impact than it actually has.

Canada's Competition Bureau expressly uses **greenwashing** for environmental claims that are deceptive because they are false, misleading, or not adequately and properly tested or substantiated where required.[^ca-guidance]

The EU describes greenwashing as misleading environmental claims and defines an **environmental claim** broadly. It includes voluntary text, pictures, graphics, symbols, labels, brand names, company names, and product names that state or imply a positive, zero, reduced, or improved environmental impact.[^eu-directive]

The UK Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) describes green claims as claims suggesting that a product, service, brand, or business provides an environmental benefit or is less harmful to the environment. A claim can mislead through untruths, hidden information, misrepresentation, or missing context.[^uk-code]

## Canada (`CA`)

### Governing rules

Canada regulates greenwashing primarily through the federal **Competition Act**:

- Section 74.01(1)(a) prohibits representations to the public that are false or misleading in a material respect.
- Section 74.01(1)(b.1) requires claims about a product's environmental benefits to be based on **adequate and proper testing**.
- Section 74.01(1)(b.2) requires claims about the environmental benefits of a business or business activity to be based on **adequate and proper substantiation**.
- The person making a claim bears the burden of proving the required testing or substantiation under sections 74.01(1)(b.1) and (b.2).[^ca-act]

The explicit environmental-claim provisions took effect on June 20, 2024. On March 26, 2026, the Budget 2025 Implementation Act, No. 1 removed the phrase requiring business-level claims to use an "internationally recognized methodology." Adequate and proper substantiation is still required.[^ca-overview]

### Enforcement test

The Bureau considers both:

1. The literal meaning of the representation.
2. Its **general impression**, including words, images, layout, qualifications, and context.

A technically true sentence can therefore be misleading if the overall presentation implies a broader or larger benefit. Fine print may not cure a misleading headline or dominant visual impression.[^ca-publication]

### Bureau compliance principles

The Bureau advises that claims should:

1. Be truthful and not false or misleading.
2. Be adequately and properly tested or substantiated where required.
3. Identify what is being compared.
4. Avoid exaggerating environmental benefits.
5. Be clear and specific rather than vague.
6. Use concrete, realistic, and verifiable plans for future goals.[^ca-guidance]

Testing and substantiation should exist **before** the claim is made. A future claim such as a net-zero target should have a credible plan, interim targets, and meaningful implementation steps rather than merely expressing an aspiration.[^ca-publication]

### Potential consequences

Canadian courts can order cessation, corrective notices, restitution in qualifying cases, and administrative monetary penalties. For a corporation, the maximum penalty can be the greater of:

- CAD 10 million for a first order or CAD 15 million for a subsequent order;
- three times the benefit derived from the conduct; or
- when that benefit cannot reasonably be determined, 3% of annual worldwide gross revenue.[^ca-penalties]

## European Union (`EU`)

### Current and upcoming framework

The EU already prohibits misleading environmental marketing under the **Unfair Commercial Practices Directive**. Directive (EU) 2024/825, commonly called the **Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition Directive**, strengthens that framework.[^eu-directive]

The directive:

- entered into force in March 2024;
- required Member States to adopt and publish implementing measures by **March 27, 2026**; and
- requires those national measures to apply from **September 27, 2026**.[^eu-dates]

The separate proposed **Green Claims Directive**, which would have established more detailed pre-verification rules, was withdrawn by the European Commission on June 20, 2025. It should not be treated as enacted law. Directive (EU) 2024/825 and existing consumer law remain the relevant EU-wide baseline.[^eu-withdrawal]

### EU definition of an environmental claim

Directive (EU) 2024/825 defines an environmental claim as a non-mandatory commercial message or representation, in any form, stating or implying that a product, category, brand, or trader:

- has a positive or zero environmental impact;
- is less damaging than alternatives; or
- has improved its environmental impact over time.[^eu-definition]

### Practices prohibited from September 27, 2026

The directive adds practices that are always considered unfair, including:

- Displaying a sustainability label that is neither established by a public authority nor based on an eligible third-party certification scheme.
- Making a generic environmental claim such as "green," "eco-friendly," or "biodegradable" without recognized excellent environmental performance relevant to the claim.
- Claiming that an entire product or business is environmentally beneficial when the claim applies only to one aspect or an unrepresentative activity.
- Claiming that a product has a neutral, reduced, or positive greenhouse-gas impact when that claim is based on offsetting emissions outside the product's value chain.
- Presenting compliance with a legal requirement applying to all products in a category as a distinctive environmental benefit.[^eu-directive]

### Claims requiring case-by-case assessment

The amended rules also target:

- future environmental-performance claims without clear, objective, public, verifiable commitments;
- plans without measurable, time-bound targets, resources, or independent expert verification;
- environmental comparisons that do not disclose their method, subjects, suppliers, and update process;
- omissions or presentations that mislead consumers about environmental characteristics, durability, repairability, or recyclability.[^eu-directive]

### Penalties

Enforcement occurs under Member State law. For certain widespread consumer-law infringements, the EU consumer enforcement framework requires national maximum fines of at least 4% of annual turnover in the affected Member State or Member States, or at least EUR 2 million where turnover information is unavailable.[^eu-penalties]

## United Kingdom (`UK`)

The UK is no longer an EU Member State and has a separate enforcement regime, but it is included because organizations commonly use "Europe" to cover both EU and UK markets.

### CMA Green Claims Code

The CMA's Green Claims Code applies established consumer-protection rules to environmental claims. Its core expectations are that claims:

1. Are truthful and accurate.
2. Are clear and unambiguous.
3. Do not omit or hide important information.
4. Make fair and meaningful comparisons.
5. consider the full lifecycle where relevant.
6. Are substantiated with up-to-date, credible evidence.[^uk-code]

The CMA's 2026 supply-chain guidance emphasizes that manufacturers, brands, distributors, retailers, and platforms can be responsible for claims they originate, repeat, or present. If a business cannot obtain enough information to verify a supplied claim, it should change or stop making the claim.[^uk-supply]

### Enforcement

The Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 gave the CMA direct consumer enforcement powers from April 2025. The CMA can determine infringements, direct corrective conduct and redress, and impose penalties without first obtaining a court order. The maximum penalty for a substantive consumer-law infringement can reach 10% of worldwide turnover.[^uk-enforcement]

|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ANY-VAGUE` | `ANY` | Vague environmental virtue claim | Shared screening principle | Uses undefined terms such as `green`, `eco-friendly`, `clean`, or `sustainable` |
| `ANY-ABSOLUTE` | `ANY` | Absolute or zero-impact environmental claim | Shared screening principle | Uses `zero impact`, `pollution-free`, `100% sustainable`, or equivalent without a complete supportable boundary |
| `ANY-SCOPE` | `ANY` | Part-to-whole or activity-to-business overstatement | Shared screening principle | Evidence covers only a component, site, process, or period but the claim implies the whole product or business |
| `ANY-EVIDENCE` | `ANY` | Missing, stale, indirect, or claim-mismatched evidence | Shared screening principle | Evidence does not support the exact wording, subject, geography, period, conditions, and magnitude |
| `ANY-OMISSION` | `ANY` | Material qualification or environmental trade-off omitted | Shared screening principle | A reasonable audience needs missing information to understand the claim accurately |
| `CA-MISLEADING` | `CA` | False or materially misleading public representation | Competition Act, section 74.01(1)(a) | Literal meaning or general impression could materially influence conduct on a false or misleading basis |
| `CA-PRODUCT-TEST` | `CA` | Product environmental-benefit claim lacks adequate and proper testing | Competition Act, section 74.01(1)(b.1) | A product claim is made without suitable testing completed before publication |
| `CA-BUSINESS-SUBSTANTIATION` | `CA` | Business or activity environmental-benefit claim lacks adequate and proper substantiation | Competition Act, section 74.01(1)(b.2) | An organization-level or activity-level claim lacks suitable supporting proof |
| `CA-COMPARISON` | `CA` | Unclear environmental comparison | Competition Bureau guidance | Comparator or extent of difference is not specific |
| `CA-FUTURE` | `CA` | Unsupported future environmental claim | Competition Bureau guidance and misleading-representation provisions | Claim lacks a concrete, realistic, verifiable plan, interim targets, or meaningful steps |
| `EU-GENERIC` | `EU` | Generic environmental claim without recognized excellent environmental performance | Directive (EU) 2024/825; applies from 2026-09-27 | Uses a generic claim such as `green`, `eco-friendly`, or `biodegradable` without qualifying performance |
| `EU-LABEL` | `EU` | Ineligible sustainability label | Directive (EU) 2024/825; applies from 2026-09-27 | Label is not established by a public authority or based on a qualifying certification scheme |

| `EU-LEGAL-MINIMUM` | `EU` | Mandatory legal requirement presented as a distinctive benefit | Directive (EU) 2024/825; applies from 2026-09-27 | Feature required for all products in the category is marketed as special |
| `EU-FUTURE` | `EU` | Misleading future environmental-performance claim | Directive (EU) 2024/825; applies from 2026-09-27 | No clear, objective, public, verifiable commitments, detailed plan, measurable targets, resources, or independent verification |
| `EU-COMPARISON` | `EU` | Environmental comparison omits material method information | Directive (EU) 2024/825; applies from 2026-09-27 | Method, compared products/suppliers, or update measures are absent |

| `UK-CLARITY` | `UK` | Unclear or ambiguous green claim | CMA Green Claims Code | Meaning, subject, boundary, or conditions are unclear |
| `UK-OMISSION` | `UK` | Important information hidden or omitted | Consumer law and CMA Green Claims Code | Missing information could affect a consumer decision |

| `UK-SUPPLY-CHAIN` | `UK` | Unverified claim originated or repeated in the supply chain | CMA 2026 supply-chain guidance | Retailer, brand, manufacturer, distributor, or platform repeats a claim it cannot verify |

## Cross-jurisdiction conclusions

The following tests are consistently important:

| Tag | Test | Compliance question |
|---|---|---|
| `ANY` | Truth | Is every express and implied statement accurate? |
| `ANY`, `CA`, `UK` | General impression | Do words, images, layout, and omissions create a broader impression than the evidence supports? |
| `ANY`, `CA`, `UK` | Evidence | Was relevant, credible evidence available before publication? |
| `ANY`, `EU`, `CA`, `UK` | Scope | Is the claim explicitly limited to the product part, process, facility, geography, or period measured? |
| `ANY`, `CA`, `UK` | Magnitude | Is a small improvement presented as a major environmental benefit? |
| `ANY`, `UK` | Lifecycle | Does the claim ignore material impacts from sourcing, production, transport, use, or disposal? |
| `ANY`, `EU`, `CA`, `UK` | Comparison | Is the baseline, comparator, method, period, and magnitude disclosed? |
| `ANY`, `EU`, `CA` | Future claims | Is there a realistic plan with resources, milestones, monitoring, and accountability? |
| `ANY`, `EU`, `UK` | Labels | Is a seal independently certified or established by a public authority? |
| `ANY`, `EU` | Offsets | Does the communication distinguish direct value-chain reductions from purchased offsets? |
